GROWTH QUALITY AND PEG
- Growth quality: compare 3-year sales CAGR, EBITDA margin over the last 8 quarters, and 3-year EPS CAGR. Volume-led growth (sales up at least 10% a year with margins steady or rising) is good. Margin-led growth (EPS far ahead of sales, margins at a multi-year high) is fragile. Growth from other income, tax or one-offs is a red flag, and so is 3-year operating cash flow below 0.7 times PAT. Say whether the last 4 quarters are speeding up or slowing against the 3-year rate.
- PEG = trailing P/E ÷ 3-year EPS CAGR in percent. Use it only when EPS grew at least 10% a year and was positive every year. Below 1 is cheap for the growth, 1 to 2 is fair, above 2 is rich unless ROCE is above 25% and the growth looks durable. Skip PEG for lenders (use P/B against ROE) and for cyclicals at peak margins (use EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle margins).
- PEG checks the price against the growth; growth quality checks that the growth is real. A "supports" verdict needs both.
